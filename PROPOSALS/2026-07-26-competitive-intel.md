## Peec AI

**What they shipped / changed:** Switched pricing from EUR to USD, live July 10, 2026 — following their New York office opening and reported $10M ARR (16 months post-launch). ([GlobeNewswire, May 28 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/28/3303009/0/en/peec-ai-hits-10m-arr-16-months-after-launch-as-brands-race-to-optimize-for-ai-search.html))

**Implication for Olokas:** Confirms Peec is doubling down on the US enterprise buyer, not the SMB tier Olokas serves. Low direct relevance this cycle.

---

## Profound (tryprofound.com)

**What they shipped / changed:** Launched **Aim** on July 2, 2026 — an always-on agent that monitors AI-answer changes, explains what changed and why, then converts flagged opportunities into scoped marketing projects with a human approval step on each one. ([digitalapplied.com](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/profound-aim-geo-marketing-agent))

**Implication for Olokas:** The most substantive move this cycle. It's the first "monitoring → scoped action → human approves" workflow shipped by a major competitor, not just a dashboard. Olokas is pure monitoring today; this pattern is a plausible next tier of feature, worth flagging for the roadmap even though Profound sells enterprise.

---

## Scrunch AI

**What they shipped / changed:** The Sitecore acquisition (announced June 3) began landing — CMS-side integration went live end of June, DAM integration targeted end of July. Scrunch is being folded into SitecoreAI Content Management and Marketing IQ rather than sold as a standalone product. ([Sitecore newsroom](https://www.sitecore.com/company/newsroom/press-releases/2026/06/sitecore-acquires-scrunch-to-help-brands-influence-discovery--and-buying-decisions))

**Implication for Olokas:** Scrunch effectively exits the standalone GEO-tool market this quarter. One fewer direct competitor at the high end, and a signal that enterprise CMS vendors treat AI-visibility as a feature to acquire, not a category to compete in directly — reinforcing that the open lane is still SMB/self-serve.

## Synthesis

Two real signals this cycle, one worth stealing, and a broader market note:

**1. "Monitoring → action" is the next feature tier.** Profound's Aim is the first shipped example of turning AI-visibility monitoring into a workflow with scoped tasks and human approval, rather than a read-only dashboard. Olokas is monitoring-only today. Not urgent to build — we don't have the scan history to make recommendations trustworthy yet — but it's the same kind of forward flag as engine-count breadth was last cycle: not a fire, but a gap that will get called out in comparison posts within 6–12 months.

**2. Enterprise GEO tooling is consolidating into adjacent platforms.** Scrunch's absorption into Sitecore is the second acquisition-style exit in this category this year (following the general pattern of CMS/analytics suites treating AI-visibility as a bolt-on). This reinforces rather than changes Olokas's thesis: the SMB self-serve tier remains uncontested by the platforms doing the consolidating.

**3. Self-run comparison content is a cheap, untested lever.** AthenaHQ's benchmark post is marketing, not science, but it's working as content — it's the kind of asset that ranks and gets cited inside AI answers about "best GEO tool." Olokas already runs a blog-outline cadence; a methodologically transparent, honestly-labeled Olokas-vs-competitors comparison could be folded into that pipeline at low cost.

**4. Capital keeps flowing into the category.** Outside the named competitor set, London-based geoSurge raised a $12M seed in early July — the largest seed round in GEO to date, with angels from Google DeepMind and Microsoft AI. Not a direct competitor yet, but a reminder that new, well-funded entrants should be expected through H2 2026.

4. **Note, don't act** — Scrunch's exit via Sitecore acquisition and geoSurge's record seed round are both market-structure signals (consolidation at the top, new capital at the bottom) rather than anything requiring a response today.
